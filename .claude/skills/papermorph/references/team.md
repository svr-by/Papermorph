# Making a chapter with a team of models

After the pilot, a chapter can be made by a coordinator handing each step of the chapter loop to a fresh subagent on the model that suits it. The chapter must reach the same bar as the book's approved chapters, in the same look (`style.json`), so it sits on the shelf beside them as one book.

Why this shape: one agent doing a whole chapter carries an ever larger context through every call, and long idle waits make that context expire and get written again; that, more than the model's price, makes a chapter expensive. Small workers that start from the files they need and finish quickly cost far less, and each step can use the cheapest model that does it well. On a physics book this cost about half as much per minute of narration as one strong agent doing everything, at the same quality.

Models below are named by tier: **small** (fast and cheap; Haiku today), **middle** (Sonnet today), **strong** (Opus today). Pass the tier's current model to the subagent: in Claude Code through the subagent's model setting, in ZCode through the worker agents in `.zcode/agents/` — `pm-small` (GLM-5.3-Flash), `pm-middle` and `pm-strong` (GLM-5.3), `pm-review` (GLM-5.3-Flash).

Not every model on a stack sees pictures. On Z.ai the flagship GLM-5.3 is text-only while GLM-5.3-Flash sees natively, so every step that looks at pictures — your own read of the storyboard sheet, Picture's self-view, Review and Rework over strips — runs on a multimodal model, or keeps the flagship and calls an image-analysis tool (`analyze_image`) as its eyes; a tool call inside the step is not the split step 3 warns against. But `analyze_image` fetches URLs only, and in practice it refuses what this workflow actually produces: a `http://localhost` URL and the CDN link that reading an image returns were both rejected with 400 (2026-10-22). The dependable eyes on Z.ai are a Flash worker's own Read of the file on disk — give vision work to Flash workers, and smoke-test `analyze_image` on one real image before building any step on it. That is why Review has its own Flash worker on Z.ai; when its reports read as rubber stamps, send the specific frames back to it to re-read, or look at them yourself.

## The coordinator

You read SKILL.md and this file, look at the storyboard sheet and the workers' replies, and pass work on. You don't write the chapter, read the page code or read workers' transcripts; keeping your own context small is most of the saving. A fresh session is the best coordinator: a long conversation rereads its whole history at every step.

Start each worker in the background with a short brief: the chapter and its pages, what to read, what to write, what not to touch, the preview URL (start one server for everyone), and a reply of a few lines. Workers don't commit. Pass work on as soon as a step returns. Meter each step: the spawn's result reports the worker's token spend — check it against the budgets under *Costs* before passing work on, and treat an outlier as a bug in the brief, not a fact of life.

## Steps

1. **Extract — small.** From `pages/chNN/text.md` and the page images it needs, write the source material into `chapters/chNN.md` in the shape of the previous chapter's: concepts in book order with the book's wording and examples, vocabulary, each figure (labels, arrows, purpose), the end-of-chapter questions (solved only when a beat will use them — the rest recorded unsolved, to be solved on demand), and a list of possible problems in the source (flagged, not decided). This file is re-read by every later worker: keep it at the previous chapter's scale, not just its shape.
2. **Narration — middle.** From chNN.md, [authoring.md](authoring.md), `style.json` and the previous approved chapter (its beat table and narration as the worked example): decide each flagged problem (`## Errata`), choose what to teach for the book's pacing (and say what was dropped), write the beat table (what the picture shows and does, each `[[mark]]` naming the word that triggers which action, where the eye goes, new visual elements needed), all questions, the narration file, then run `tts.py` and confirm every mark is in timings.js. The plan has to be concrete enough for a different model to build from. A review round is a fresh small job: send the user's notes to a new worker with a surgical brief — the exact edits and the files to touch. Resuming the original worker re-pays its whole context on every call and can cost more than the first draft did.
3. **Picture** — strong for tiers 1–2; for tier 0 the small multimodal builder (intake: tier 0 works on the smallest model — and a builder that sees can judge its own frames), escalating to strong only for rework its report can't cover. From the plan, `style.json`, `assets/templates/chapter.html` and the book's library: storyboard layout first, then motion, camera and questions, new shared elements in the library, a quick self-view of the busiest beats and lint. The previous approved chapter's page is for studying shape — head, one lesson beat, one question, the finish — not for reading wholesale, and `engine.js` is never read: `engine.md` is the interface. Carry the book's engine gotchas into the brief, one line each, so they are not rediscovered from scratch. Big files go down in sections with a tool call between — a long silent generation can trip the session's inactivity reaper and kill the run, and a resumed run re-pays everything already spent. The plan is a plan, not a cage. Storyboard and motion stay in one step: split, the next worker would have to read the whole page again.
   - Look at the storyboard sheet yourself when it comes back; problems visible there are cheapest to name now.
4. **Checks — small, report-only**, in parallel with 5: lint, `check_blank.py`, `overlap.py`, `still.py`, `perf.py` in Firefox, and an e2e test adapted from the previous chapter's, in Chromium and Firefox. Instruments first, eyes second: sheets and instrument output decide most frames; open full-size captures only where an instrument flagged. It reports problems with beat and time and changes only its own test.
5. **Review — middle**, in parallel with 4: watch every beat as [review.md](review.md) describes, against the storyboard and the approved chapters. It fixes small local problems itself and lists larger ones with beat, time and a suggested fix. Its reply opens with the beats it watched; if some are missing, send it back to finish — continuing is cheaper only while the remaining work outweighs the context the resumed run re-reads; for anything bigger, start fresh from its notes. Bound its reading — shot sheets, strips and a checklist first, full-size frames only for disputed moments, about ten to a chapter — and have it append per-beat notes to a file as it goes, so a killed run resumes from notes instead of from zero. An unbounded pass over every frame of a chapter costs millions of tokens.
6. **Rework — strong, only for real problems** left by 4 and 5: a brief listing exactly those, re-checked with strips, `still.py`, lint and e2e in both browsers.
7. **Register — small.** Give it the exact edits: the contents entry, the previous chapter's `CHAPTER.next`, cover or shelf counts, `chapters.md` and the status line in chNN.md. Then lint and e2e.

## Page mode

A page chapter is cheap enough to skip the team most of the time: one **Build** worker (middle; strong for a hard subject) reads `chNN.md`, `style.json` and one previous approved chapter, writes the sections narration and the page in one pass ([page.md](page.md)), runs lint and fixes what it finds — about 0.3–1M tokens. The coordinator settles the plan and the section texts with the user before that worker starts (audio is the only costly step), then reviews the page in the browser ([review.md](review.md#page-mode)) and registers. When the book's quality bar wants a second pair of eyes, one **Review** worker (middle) walks the same checklist. Extract and Register stay as above; there is no storyboard, motion, checks or rework step.

## Costs

Budgets are per worker, for the small/middle/strong mapping above, measured on algebra9 (2026-10-23/24, Z.ai GLM-5.3 / GLM-5.3-Flash): narration 0.8–1.5M, a revision round 0.3M, checks 0.7–2.6M, review 0.8–7M. The two that keep exploding, and what actually helps: **Extract** runs 2–3.3M whenever formulas must be verified against page images — the PNG reads dominate; brief it to open only pages it doubts and keep the extract at the previous chapter's scale (a 67 KB extract re-read by every later worker is the most expensive mistake a chapter can make). **Picture** is the worst: 8.5M rebuilding ch01, 21.5M building ch02, both from reading two full reference chapters and then paying the growing context on ~100 tool calls. So: give the builder ONE reference chapter, not two (the library `figs.js` already carries the shared devices); tell it to write in chunks of 3–5 beats per call (fewer calls = fewer context re-payments; the idle reaper allows ~9 minutes of quiet, a 3–5-beat chunk fits); forbid re-reading its own finished page — verify by lint and targeted greps. A four-note rework ran 2.9M with before/after frames and the full e2e matrix, half that with after-frames only and one browser. A chapter that stays near these budgets lands around 5M tokens end to end. When a worker comes back over budget, fix the brief, not just the number.

## Before delivering

Check the reports against each other (an odd claim is worth one look at the file), confirm lint and e2e pass, and deliver the chapter URL with `?review`. If the user wants the cost, it is in the subagent transcripts (`subagents/agent-<id>.jsonl` under the session's folder: `~/.claude/projects/` in Claude Code, `~/.zcode/cli/` in ZCode; usage per assistant message, each message id counted once) plus your own session's usage.
